Discussion:

I think reading the second article and remembering everything about how the internet and how it operates and connects with others online in different locations and then reading the first article puts things in perspective because of how we usually imagine these cables and how we’re able to access content is mainly done through satellites or through transmission lines above ground. When in reality a lot of the heavy lifting and main work is done through power cables underground / underwater and what the author is trying to show to everyday people is how these cables look and how they play a larger part in our everyday life.

I remember reading about Invisible Infrastructure such as underwater transmission lines. I think they are called submarine power cables but in another class they told us about how these transmission lines allow us to communicate and consume content from different countries across the world. Learning now that these underwater cables are more common than what we previously thought is interesting. It does make sense that water and gas lines that are located underground because if they were above then things would become chaotic in case that someone accidentally damages them. It is also interesting looking through the manhole covers and seeing how there are different covers for either the different companies and the different networks being held underground. When I am walking through the city I never really paid any attention to the manholes, In my head I always assumed that they would just be covers for people to go into the sewers and there would be nothing but just water underneath but now looking at the underground network of NYC it makes sense that it would be home to an underground network that helps power and move everything in the city.
