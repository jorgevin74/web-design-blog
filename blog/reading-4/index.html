Discussion:

In the first reading, Another Network is Possible, we learn that a new network titled IndyMedia.org which was created because of the Seattle WTO protests going on in November 1999. From this website that garnered 1.5 Million people in their first week which rivaled other pages such as CNN and CBS. It created a whole different network of IndyMedia websites that were inspired by this movement and many others created their own based on the state / country they lived in. I think it is so interesting how many people were able to deliver the right news to audiences that may have to worry if there's misinformation being spread by big media corporations and such and I think that a whole network of IndyMedia websites that were created shortly after really helped bring movmements to the forefront of these pages and how they can get involved and help make a difference for their community. This is something that has been sort of lost as the times change, now with bigger social media platforms there are a lot of communities such like this available although as mentioned in the article, As IndyMedia began to grow into something bigger and more people visited their site, they were subject to trolls coming in and spewing hate. I do think as to how this was only in 1999 and how it is possible for another network as big as IndyMedia could come in and take control and with social media being as prevelant as it's ever been and with the world getting scarier and crazier, one could only hope that we are able to create that safespace or rather a newer network building upon what IndyMedia started.

In the second reading, Architechting Emergence, the authors goes through the ideas of what systems are, how we are able to contribute to these systems with examples from drawings all the way to getting a quote from yoda and then coming with one word to fill in the blank from the previous word that another user wrote down. I think it is interesting to see how people can essentially come up with their own "worlds" of sorts and implement them wherever they see fit. I think it's even more interesting going into the last paragraph where they talk about creating our own world and how that would look like and many users have already came up with their own ideas for it. Eventually leading into the idea of what Zhenzhen Qi & Yang Wang are trying to push in that we should create a virtual world in an art museum. The project they made is called ThingThingThing it allows people to contribute and add in their own projects and almost leave a piece of their world and their own ideas into this one almost as if they were leaving a landmark on a newly discovered planet.It does also lead into our project of creating a space where many others can come in and leave a message of sorts that'll be seen by many others. I think this way it allow for artworks to stick around and in case they want to explore where this comes from and why, then they can actually use this museum / world to discover all sorts of different things.

In the third reading, The woman who taught internet strangers to actually care for one another, we learn that a woman named Stacy Horn started an online community called Echo -- "The East Coast Hang-Out" which was created long before the World Wide Web and at a certain point back when Women only made up a tenth of the online population, Echo's user base was 40 percent female and it was essentially a hang out where they can talk between themselves and report any harrasement coming their way. Connecting this to now more modern social media platforms such as Instagram and Twitter, or formerly known as twitter, where there are communities within this big social space but a lot of it doesn't hit the same if that makes sense. I feel like social media tries to replicate that safe sense feeling of harboring a small amount of people inside of a community that like a certain type of media whether it be a books, movies, etc. A lot of these bigger platforms also sell their data and have many ads in placed that are catered to what you search for and like and I feel that with Echo where Stacy didn't sell out really gave people a community and a new home of sorts.
